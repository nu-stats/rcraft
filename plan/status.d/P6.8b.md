## P6.8b: resampling leftovers, 2026-11-24

- **Done** (`crates/catalog/src/resampling.rs`, `crates/engine/tests/resampling.rs`, two new tests, R 4.4.3 with boot 1.3.31, no new packages):
  - Clusters and strata: rows within strata are `boot(..., strata =)`; clusters are drawn as rows of a one-column frame of cluster names (with the stratum of each cluster as `strata`; a cluster in two strata is refused), and every row of a drawn cluster comes with it. The draws equal `boot.array`'s and each replicate is redone by hand. On forty rows in eight equal clusters (four per stratum), the standard errors of the mean (clusters, clusters within strata, rows within strata) lie within four Monte Carlo errors of their exact bootstrap values (the between-cluster variance over G, summed over the strata with weights (G_h / G)^2), and so do Stata's `bootstrap r(mean), reps(20000) cluster() strata()`; for `regress` with `cluster()` and for `r(sd)/r(mean)` the dialog's and Stata's errors agree within the combined Monte Carlo error (taken from the replicates' kurtosis).
  - Intervals: basic = 2 t0 less the upper and lower limits interpolated on the normal scale; studentised = t0 less the estimate's own error times the interpolated quantiles of (t* - t0) / se* (the mean's var / n, or a coefficient's least-squares variance). Stata's intervals: normal = estimate plus or minus z times the bootstrap error (no bias correction, unlike boot's); percentile and bias-corrected (z0 = qnorm of the share of replicates at or below the estimate, limits at pnorm(2 z0 plus or minus z)) taken by Stata's percentile rule (the replicate at the next whole position, or the average of two when n p is whole). All three equal `bstat` with `estat bootstrap, all` on the dialog's own replicates to 1e-9, at 999 and at 1000 replicates (at 1000 boot's percentile limits differ from Stata's by part of a replicate).
  - A statistic of one's own: an R expression evaluated `with()` the resampled rows of the listed columns; several numbers keep their names; something that is not a number stops with a plain message.
  - Permutation within strata: exact when the product of each stratum's arrangements is at most B (each stratum's combinations crossed), otherwise each stratum shuffled with `sample.int`. On twenty rows in two strata the 63504 arrangements equal a listing by crossed sums, and Stata's `permute, strata()` lower and upper p-values lie within four Monte Carlo errors of the exact ones.
  - Regression coefficient: the predictor is permuted against the outcome and the other predictors and refitted with `lm.fit`; exact over n! orders when n! is at most B. On seven rows all 5040 orders equal an independent listing, and Stata's p-values lie within its Monte Carlo error of the exact ones; on twenty rows (with and without strata) the dialog's and Stata's agree within the combined error. The observed coefficient equals Stata's to its single precision.
  - p-values: two-sided (as large in size, as before), one-sided as large or as small, or Stata's two-sided rule (twice the smaller one-sided p, at most 1).
- **Refused, with the reason in the dialog**: a studentised interval for a median, a correlation or one's own statistic (no per-replicate variance) and with clusters (the variance would have to allow for them).
- **Differences from Stata** (small-fixes rows 20 and 21, fixed): Stata's normal interval is not bias-corrected and its percentiles follow its own rule; its two-sided permutation p is twice the smaller one-sided one. Both are offered as choices. Stata's `permute` gives count / B where the dialog gives (count + 1) / (B + 1); they agree within the Monte Carlo error.
