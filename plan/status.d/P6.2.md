## P6.2: Multilevel models, second half, 2026-11-24

- **Done** (`crates/catalog/src/multilevel.rs`, `crates/engine/tests/multilevel.rs`, glmmTMB added to `ci.yml`):
  - Mixed model (glmmTMB): `glmmTMB::glmmTMB` with `stats::gaussian`, `binomial`, `poisson` or `glmmTMB::nbinom2`, ML, default control (tighter tolerances only add a harmless "singular convergence" warning). Coefficients, variance components and log-likelihood equal Stata to 1e-5 or better: `mixed` (ML), `melogit` and `mepoisson` and `menbreg` all with `intmethod(laplace)`; Stata's `ln(alpha)` is minus the log of glmmTMB's theta. The zero-inflated Poisson log-likelihood equals the Laplace approximation written out in plain R (1e-7), and is a maximum of it. Stata has no `intpoints(1)` for these models (it is only allowed with `intmethod(mcaghermite)`), so `intmethod(laplace)` is the check.
  - Intraclass correlation: variance of the group intercepts over the total, cumulative for nested levels (`school`, `school>class`), residual pi^2/3 for a logistic and 1 for a probit model, intercept variance only with random slopes (Stata's "conditional on zero values"). Equal to `estat icc` (1e-6 to 2e-5). Counts, crossed groups and non-lme4 models are refused with the reason, as Stata refuses them.
  - Random slopes in the logistic and Poisson model (`(1 + x || g)` or `(1 + x | g)`), equal to `melogit` and `mepoisson` with independent and unstructured covariance (coefficients, variances, covariance, log-likelihood). A logistic fit with random slopes did not converge in Stata on 98 pupils, so these checks use 633 pupils in 40 schools (centred hours).
  - Crossed groups in both lme4 dialogs: linear equals `mixed ... || _all: R.a || b:` and the likelihood written out in plain R; logistic equals `melogit` with `intmethod(laplace)` on 98 and 633 rows.
  - "Save random-effect predictions": columns `u_<group>` and `u_<slope>_<group>` with each group's conditional mode for every row (also the rows left out for a missing predictor, as Stata's `predict` does). Equal to `predict, reffects` for the linear models (slope, nested) and to `predict, remodes` for the logistic one (plain `reffects` after `melogit` gives posterior means, not modes).
- **Small differences** (noted here; `plan/small-fixes.md` untouched):
  - glmmTMB's standard errors come from the full Hessian: for the Gaussian model they differ from Stata's `mixed` (generalised least squares) by about 1e-3, for the negative binomial from `menbreg` by about 2e-3; Poisson and logistic agree to 1e-6.
  - `glmer`'s standard errors are conditional on the variances: with crossed groups they differ from `melogit`'s by up to 5% (random slopes agree to 1e-4).
  - Stata's test data were read with `hours` as a float, which moves its log-likelihoods by about 4e-6, so the checks that depend on it use 1e-7.
