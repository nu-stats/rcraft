## P6.5: Time series, second half, 2026-11-25

  - ARIMA: seasonal AR and MA orders, seasonal differences and the season length, multiplied in as Stata's `sarima(P, D, Q, s)`; exogenous variables as Stata's `arima y x` (with differences the variables are differenced too, as Stata does: `arima y x, arima(1,1,0)` is `arima D.y D.x, ar(1)`). Equal to Stata 19 with tight tolerances: coefficients to 1e-7, errors to 1e-5, log-likelihood to 1e-12 (`arima visits, arima(1,0,0) sarima(1,0,0,4)`, `sarima(0,1,1,4)`, `arima load temp, ar(1)` and its differenced form), and to the exact likelihood written out from the autocovariances. Level forecasts after any differences undo them one step at a time, with errors from the psi weights of the multiplied-out polynomials; equal to Stata's `predict, y dynamic()` to 1e-8 after a seasonal difference (Stata's `mse` there is the differenced series', as with ordinary differences). A model with exogenous variables gets no forecast (the dialog says why). The model now keeps the series it was fitted to as `x`, which forecast::forecast needs.
  - Unit-root test: the Dickey-Fuller Z(t) by `urca::ur.df` (none, constant, trend; lags) equals `dfuller` to 1e-10; Phillips-Perron is written out from the regression on the last value with the Bartlett-weighted autocovariances (Hamilton 17.6) and equals `pperron`'s Z(rho) and Z(t) to 1e-12, Stata's default lags int(4 (T/100)^(2/9)) included (`urca::ur.pp` is about 1e-3 off Stata, so it is not used). The p-value is MacKinnon's 1994 response surface from his published coefficients, equal to Stata's to 1e-13, including 1 and 0 past his bounds; none without a constant, as Stata.
  - Exponential smoothing by `stats::HoltWinters`: Stata's start values (the mean of the first floor(N/2) rows; the line fitted to them for the trend models) are put before the series so HoltWinters' first forecast is Stata's of row 1. Single, Brown's double (as Holt's with alpha (2 - alpha) and alpha / (2 - alpha)) and Holt-Winters equal `tssmooth exponential`, `dexponential` and `hwinters` with given parameters to 1e-12 (every one-step forecast and the forecasts past the end) and to the recursions written out; fitted parameters equal Stata's to 1e-4 (Stata's search stops near the flat least-squares minimum; the dialog's is at least as low). Stata's reported rss is about 2e-9 relative from the sum over its own series, so it is held to 1e-8.
  - Vector autoregression by `vars::VAR`: coefficients, Stata's large-sample errors (lm's times sqrt(df / T)), log-likelihood, AIC, SBIC and det(Sigma) equal `var` to 1e-12 and least squares written out; orthogonalised impulse responses from `vars::Phi` and the Cholesky factor of the ML covariance equal `irf table oirf` to 1e-10 (vars' own `irf` uses the df-divided covariance).
  - Forecast (Objects > Model, on `Arima` and `HoltWinters` objects): `forecast::forecast` with h steps and an interval; its forecasts and errors equal `predict, dynamic()` and `mse` for an ARMA and a seasonal model to 1e-7, and `tssmooth hwinters, forecast()` to 1e-12. A model with exogenous variables is refused with the reason.
  - Time-series plot: one or more series in time order by `stats::ts.plot`, with a legend; draws exactly one real picture.
